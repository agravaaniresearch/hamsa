How Hamsa works

Four steps, every time you open Hacker News. None of them sends anything to an AI.

  1. 1 · Reads the pagesFive pages of Hacker News, merged into one list.
  2. 2 · Sweeps out the noiseFlame wars, insults, repeats and one-liners are folded away.
  3. 3 · Matches what you loveScores each story against your bookmarks, likes and reading.
  4. 4 · Shows the best, and something newYour keeps first, plus a few discoveries you would not have found.

Discover and likes

The stories you would not have found, and how you teach Hamsa.

In plain words: on every front page, Hamsa sets aside a few stories that match nothing you have ever saved, but that other readers clearly find worth reading. That is the Discover section, and it comes first. Thumbs up and down on any story or comment teach Hamsa your taste; stories you upvote on HN count as likes too.

Discoveries per front page0 switches Discover off.
  • New to you: most of its words appear nowhere in your bookmarks, opens or likes.
  • Worth a look: rising points, a healthy number of comments, a respected submitter.
  • A new site you have never saved, or an emerging topic several people posted about today.

Remember what I open

Stories and links you open are remembered in this browser and used as a quiet signal. The log can be downloaded to train a small AI of your own on your choices.

Blocklist

Topics you never want to see.

In plain words: a list of subjects to hide, like start-up funding news. Hidden stories are put aside, never deleted, and a story you would clearly love still gets through. Describe what you dislike in your own words and Hamsa can draft the list for you.

Drafting uses your AI key once. Testing changes nothing; it only shows what each group would have hidden today.

Scoring

Where the lines are drawn.

In plain words: every story and comment gets a mark from 0 to 1. Above the keep line it is shown first; between the lines it waits in maybe; below the floor it is set aside. Most people never need to touch these.

The mark is 1 − e^(−2 × total ÷ ceiling), where the total adds up every reason shown under "Why this score".

AI summaries

Optional. Only when you press Summarise.

In plain words: if you add a key from an AI company, Hamsa can write a short summary of a long discussion, using only the comments that matter to you. You pay the AI company directly, usually less than a rupee a time, and Hamsa shows you the cost each time.

Backup and sync

Keep everything safe, and take it with you.

In plain words: your settings, likes, dislikes and blocklist can be saved as one file, or kept in step across your computers automatically. Your AI keys and tokens stay private unless you tick the box to include them.

A backup file

One JSON file (an open format). Restore it in any browser that runs Hamsa.

Firefox Sync

Automatic, through your Firefox account, on every Firefox where you are signed in. No keys, no reading log.
